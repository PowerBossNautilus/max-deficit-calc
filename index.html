Body fat estimate uses the US Navy circumference method — waist, neck (and hip for women), plus height. No special equipment needed beyond a tape measure; typical error is around ±3–4 percentage points versus DEXA. Measure waist at the navel and neck just below the larynx, relaxed, not sucked in.

Reading the dial — X=13 assumes a narrower margin of safety and pushes toward a faster, more aggressive rate; X=20 assumes a wider margin and keeps the deficit conservatively within what fat stores alone can supply. This is a modeling estimate, not a medical recommendation — actual composition of weight lost also depends on training, protein intake, and sleep.

Why the rate isn't constant — weekly fat loss equals current fat mass ÷ X, so as fat mass drops the same X yields a smaller weekly amount — fat mass decays geometrically rather than linearly. Lean mass is held constant throughout; if a goal implies losing more fat mass than you have, or your target isn't below your current values, no estimate is shown.
